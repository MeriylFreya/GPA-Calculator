CGPA vs SGPA: Complete Comparison

Understanding the difference between Cumulative and Semester GPA is essential for tracking your academic progress. Let's break it down.

Quick Comparison

Aspect SGPA (Semester GPA) CGPA (Cumulative GPA)
Full Form Semester Grade Point Average Cumulative Grade Point Average
Coverage Single semester only All semesters combined
When it's used End of each semester Overall academic record
Formula Sum of (Grade Points × Credits) ÷ Total Credits Average of all semester SGPAs
Impact Shows performance in one semester Shows overall academic standing
Employer View Less important More important for jobs

SGPA: Semester Grade Point Average

SGPA measures your academic performance for a single semester. It reflects your grade point average for the courses you've taken in that specific semester.

SGPA Formula

SGPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credit Weightage) ÷ Σ(Credit Weightage)

When SGPA is Used

  • Evaluating semester-specific performance
  • Determining eligibility for semester-based scholarships
  • Merit-based course selections
  • Semester-based probation decisions
  • Short-term academic progress

SGPA Example

Let's say you took 4 subjects in a semester:

  • Math: Grade 8.5, Credits 4 → (8.5 × 4) = 34
  • Physics: Grade 9.0, Credits 3 → (9.0 × 3) = 27
  • Chemistry: Grade 7.5, Credits 3 → (7.5 × 3) = 22.5
  • English: Grade 8.0, Credits 2 → (8.0 × 2) = 16

SGPA = (34 + 27 + 22.5 + 16) ÷ (4 + 3 + 3 + 2) = 99.5 ÷ 12 = 8.29

CGPA: Cumulative Grade Point Average

CGPA is your overall GPA across all semesters of your academic program. It gives a comprehensive view of your academic performance throughout your entire course.

CGPA Formula

CGPA = (SGPA₁ + SGPA₂ + SGPA₃ + ... + SGPAₙ) ÷ Number of Semesters

When CGPA is Used

  • Job applications and interviews
  • Higher education admissions
  • Competitive exam eligibility
  • Overall academic standing
  • Fellowship and scholarship applications
  • Final degree classification

CGPA Example

If your SGPAs across semesters are:

  • Semester 1: SGPA = 8.25
  • Semester 2: SGPA = 8.40
  • Semester 3: SGPA = 8.35
  • Semester 4: SGPA = 8.50

CGPA = (8.25 + 8.40 + 8.35 + 8.50) ÷ 4 = 33.5 ÷ 4 = 8.375

Why Both Matter

SGPA is Important Because:

  • Immediate feedback on semester performance
  • Helps identify areas needing improvement
  • Determines eligibility for next semester or promotions
  • Shows trending improvement or decline
  • Useful for internal university decisions

CGPA is More Important Because:

  • Comprehensive reflection of your entire academic career
  • What employers primarily look at
  • Required for most competitive opportunities
  • Indicates consistent performance over time
  • Required for higher education/postgraduate studies

How to Improve Your CGPA

  • Focus on upcoming semesters: Higher SGPAs in future semesters will gradually improve your CGPA
  • Understand the weightage: Know which courses have higher credit values
  • Monitor progress: Use our calculator frequently to track your CGPA
  • Consistent effort: Maintain or improve SGPA each semester
  • Study smart: Prioritize based on credits and your current standings

Common Questions

Can CGPA be lowered?

Yes, if your SGPA in upcoming semesters is lower than your current CGPA, it will bring your overall CGPA down. However, if you maintain or improve SGPA, your CGPA will stabilize or improve.

Which GPA do employers care about?

Employers primarily focus on CGPA as it represents your overall academic standing. However, a strong SGPA in final semesters can also be favorable.

Is 8.0 CGPA good?

A CGPA of 8.0+ is generally considered good. However, "good" depends on your institution, industry, and goals. Many top companies accept 7.0+ CGPA.

How often is SGPA calculated?

SGPA is typically calculated at the end of each semester. The exact timing depends on your university's examination schedule.

Key Takeaways

  • CGPA is the cumulative average across all semesters
  • Both are important but for different purposes
  • CGPA matters more for career and education opportunities
  • You can improve CGPA by maintaining higher SGPAs going forward
